Why we exist

From the screen to the room

People are more online, more alone and less in church than at any time in living memory. We think those three facts belong together, and that the answer is older than any of them.

Belong to a church

70%→47%

U.S. adults who belong to a church, synagogue or mosque, 1999 to 2020. Below half for the first time in Gallup’s records. Gallup, 2021

In a pew most weeks

42%→30%

Adults who attend services weekly or almost weekly, 2001–03 to 2021–23. Gallup, 2024

Hours on a screen

7 hrsa day

Average daily screen time for U.S. adults online, about a third of waking life. DataReportal, 2025

Lonely

1 in 2adults

About half of U.S. adults report loneliness. The Surgeon General called it an epidemic, with health risks on a par with smoking. U.S. Surgeon General, 2023

Teens persistently sad or hopeless

30%→40%

High-school students who felt so sad or hopeless for two weeks or more that they stopped usual activities, 2013 to 2023. CDC Youth Risk Behavior Survey

The church was never the problem

Every number above points the same way: more time alone with a glowing rectangle, less time in a room with people who know your name. The local church is one of the last places in American life where people of every age and income sit together every week, sing together, eat together, carry one another’s burdens and are missed when they’re gone.

Jesus didn’t save individuals into isolation. He called a people, and he told them to be the light of the world, a city on a hill that cannot be hidden. A light needs a lamp-stand. That’s a local body of believers.

Faith is already on the screen

In under twenty years social media went from almost no one to most American adults. Services, Scripture and Christian music are on screens too.

Use social media

5%→72%

U.S. adults who use at least one social media site, 2005 to 2021. About half did by 2011. Pew Research Center, 2021

Christian music streams

+18.5%in 2025

Growth in U.S. streams of Christian and gospel music in 2025, against 4.6% for all music. Luminate, 2025 year-end report

Read Scripture with an app or website

21%of adults

U.S. adults who use apps or websites to help them read the Bible or other scriptures. Pew Research Center, 2023

Watched a service online or on TV

36%→24%

U.S. adults who watched religious services online or on TV in the past month, July 2020 to November 2022. Pew Research Center, 2022 and 2023

But the screen hasn’t held people. The share watching services online or on TV fell from 36% in July 2020 to 24% by November 2022. A feed can’t bring a meal when you’re sick or notice when you’re missing.

What we do about it

  1. Experience Jesus. A creator shares one link. Someone hears the gospel, in the creator’s voice or ours, and responds.
  2. Grow. They start a first discipleship course, and someone follows up with them by name.
  3. Belong. We point them toward a healthy local church and walk with them until they’re in one. That is the finish line, not the view count.

Social media is where people are. It is not where they should stay. We use the screen to get people into the room.
